# Special education scope
The long-term academic direction is a Bachelor of Arts in Special Education with emphasis on Mild to Moderate Exceptionalities. This is a research and engineering direction, not a claim of a degree, license or professional endorsement.

Priorities: inclusive instruction; literacy and mathematics access; executive function; communication/language; assistive technology; cognitive development; educator adaptations; family participation; and educational accessibility. Terminology, eligibility frameworks and professional practice differ by jurisdiction and should be reviewed using current authoritative sources.

Research records must describe the studied population and setting; never assume results generalize to every learner with a diagnostic label. Document the range of learner needs, participant exclusion, outcome measures, implementation conditions, harms and contradictory evidence. Co-design with educators and people with disabilities, compensate participation when possible, and preserve agency.

The retained LearnOS reading module offers declarative activities and multiple evidence modes. It is a curriculum prototype requiring educator review, especially because its grade/standard mapping may need correction. Linking a paper to it does not turn it into a validated intervention.

Excluded: automated disability diagnosis, eligibility or placement decisions, unreviewed high-stakes recommendations and individualized education plan automation. AI may assist research understanding and draft materials with human review; professionals retain responsibility. Formal participant research must obtain appropriate ethics/consent review before collecting data.
